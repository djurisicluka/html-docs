Issuer rulebook

Conservative planning guardrails—not guarantees. Always read the exact application terms.

Chase

  • Most Chase cards are subject to the informal 5/24 rule.
  • Chase business cards usually require being below 5/24 but generally do not add to 5/24 when absent from personal reports.
  • Space Chase applications; roughly 90 days is a safer working cadence than stacking applications.
  • Sapphire eligibility wording changes. Read the live product-specific bonus language before applying.
Hard: 5/24 behavior. Soft: velocity and total Chase exposure.

American Express

  • Welcome-offer eligibility is commonly once per product/lifetime unless the offer omits lifetime language.
  • The application pop-up is the strongest real-time signal; cancel before acceptance if it says no bonus.
  • Personal Platinum history does not automatically equal Business Platinum history; they are distinct products.
  • Common credit-card velocity guidance: about 1 approval per 5 days and 2 per 90 days; charge cards may behave differently.
Your known personal Platinum history is flagged below.

Citi

  • Common velocity guardrails: 1 application per 8 days and 2 per 65 days.
  • Business applications are often spaced about 90 days.
  • Bonus clocks vary by product/family and can be based on opening or closing; read the exact terms.
Do not assume every ThankYou or AAdvantage card shares one clock.

Capital One

  • Capital One can be sensitive to many open accounts and recent inquiries.
  • Use the issuer's pre-approval flow when available before taking a hard pull.
  • Product-specific prior-bonus and current-card restrictions matter more than old universal folklore.
Your two legacy Capital One products remain unidentified.

Bank of America

  • Common approval guidance: 2 cards in 2 months, 3 in 12 months, and 4 in 24 months.
  • New-account sensitivity is often described as 3/12 without a deposit relationship and 7/12 with one.
  • Alaska/Atmos product eligibility terms should be checked card by card.
Relationship banking can materially change outcomes.

Barclays / Wells / U.S. Bank

  • Barclays is often sensitive to many recent accounts and weak card usage.
  • Wells Fargo welcome-offer spacing and product restrictions vary; verify the current terms.
  • U.S. Bank is commonly inquiry/new-account sensitive, especially for premium products.
For all three, low recent velocity materially helps.
